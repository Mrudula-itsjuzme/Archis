# Product Thesis

Architecture software is increasingly good at representing buildings, coordinating views, checking rules and generating alternatives.

Archis is testing a different missing layer: **revision memory**.

When an architect authors a first draft, the geometry is visible but the reasons behind it are only partially explicit. Later requirements force the design to change. A valid revision can still destroy the original spatial idea.

Archis therefore treats the first draft as evidence, not a blank canvas.

1. Reconstruct a semantic building model.
2. Propose hypotheses about relationships that appear intentional.
3. Show confidence and rationale rather than pretending certainty.
4. Let the architect protect, ignore or correct those hypotheses.
5. Receive a concrete change request.
6. Search locally around the architect's design.
7. Reject invalid candidates.
8. Compare remaining candidates by semantic disruption.
9. Explain what each revision changes, preserves and weakens.
10. Let the architect decide and learn from that correction.

## Product promise, if the hypothesis survives validation

**Change the requirement without unnecessarily changing the idea.**

## Initial user

Small architecture studios working through early residential/concept revisions, where one architect is still close enough to the design intent to correct the system quickly.

## Initial adoption strategy

Do not require a full BIM migration. Begin as a reasoning/revision workspace around an imported or reconstructed early plan and hand approved changes back into the professional workflow.

## What would make this a company rather than a feature?

Only evidence can answer that. The company case becomes stronger if:

- revision reasoning recurs frequently across projects
- architects value persistent project intent, not only one-off generation
- corrections create useful proprietary training/evaluation data
- semantic impact analysis expands naturally into cost, code, collaboration and downstream coordination
- the engine can integrate with existing authoring environments without losing its core advantage

If those conditions do not appear, Archis should remain a narrower tool, integration, or research project rather than being inflated into a platform story.
